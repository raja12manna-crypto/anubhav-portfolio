Case study · Take-home for Oliv.AI · 2026

Teaching an AI coach what good sounds like.

Oliv's Coach agent scores sales calls. But a score only means something if managers agree on the bar. This take-home designs the flow where a sales manager defines a reusable coaching rubric — and the launch section that announces it.

01 · The problem

An AI score is only as fair as the rubric behind it.

Managers coach on a handful of calls they happen to hear, each judged by a slightly different bar. An AI coach can listen to every call — but only if someone tells it, precisely, what a weak and a strong answer sound like. If that step is tedious or vague, managers skip it, and the AI's scores become noise nobody trusts.

02 · Key decisions

Four decisions that shape the flow.

DECISION 01

Managers write the extremes. AI drafts the middle.

Defining all five levels from scratch is where people give up. The manager writes what a 1 and a 5 sound like; Coach drafts the bands between, in the manager's own words — to be edited, not accepted blindly.

DECISION 02

"Not observed" is an answer.

If a skill never came up on a call, Coach says so instead of guessing a score. An AI that admits absence is easier to trust than one that always has a number.

DECISION 03

Rubrics are versioned.

Once calls are scored against a rubric, editing it would silently change what old scores mean. Versioning keeps a score from March meaning what it meant in March — and the builder locks while scoring is live.

DECISION 04

Mobile is recomposed, not scaled.

For the launch section, the mobile layout leads with the core idea and turns the scored-call illustration into a readable proof list, instead of shrinking the desktop design.
